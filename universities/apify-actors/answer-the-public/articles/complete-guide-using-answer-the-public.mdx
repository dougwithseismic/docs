## Running Your First Search

### Step-by-Step Basic Search

**Step 1: Choose Your Keyword**

Good keywords for ATP:
- ✅ "email marketing" (broad topic)
- ✅ "coffee maker" (product)
- ✅ "content writing" (service)
- ✅ "SEO" (industry)

Poor keywords for ATP:
- ❌ "how to make coffee" (already a question)
- ❌ "best email marketing software for small businesses" (too specific)
- ❌ "the ultimate guide to SEO" (article title, not search term)

**Step 2: Select Location and Language**

For US-focused content:
- Country: United States
- Language: English

For international content:
- Choose target market country
- Select appropriate language

**Step 3: Choose Data Source (Pro/Expert)**

- **Google**: General search behavior, most comprehensive
- **YouTube**: Video content ideas
- **TikTok**: Trending short-form video topics
- **Instagram**: Visual content and social trends
- **Amazon**: Product research, e-commerce
- **Bing**: Alternative to Google (sometimes different results)
- **ChatGPT**: AI-informed query patterns

**Step 4: Run Search**

Click "Search" button and wait 5-15 seconds for results.

### What the Results Show

ATP organizes results into categories:

**Questions:**
- Organized by question word (what, how, why, where, when, which, who, etc.)
- "What is email marketing?"
- "How to start email marketing?"

**Prepositions:**
- Shows contextual searches
- "email marketing for beginners"
- "email marketing with Mailchimp"
- "email marketing to customers"

**Comparisons:**
- Competitive searches
- "email marketing vs social media"
- "email marketing or content marketing"

**Alphabetical:**
- Related searches A-Z
- "email marketing agency"
- "email marketing best practices"
- "email marketing calendar"

**Related:**
- Associated topics and themes
- Broader context around your keyword

## Understanding the Visualizations

### The Wheel Chart Explained

ATP's iconic visualization shows questions radiating from the center:

**Visual Elements:**

**Center:** Your seed keyword
**Inner Ring:** Question words (what, how, why, etc.)
**Outer Ring:** Specific questions
**Colors:** Different question types
**Size:** Not indicative of volume (all questions equal size)

**How to Read It:**

1. **Scan for patterns**: Which question types dominate?
2. **Look for clusters**: Related questions group together
3. **Identify outliers**: Unique questions that stand out
4. **Note gaps**: Question types with few results (opportunity areas)

**Common Misconception:**
<Warning>
The wheel chart does NOT show search volume or popularity. All questions appear equally sized regardless of how many people search for them. You'll need separate tools (Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs, SEMrush) to validate search volumes.
</Warning>

### List View vs Wheel View

**When to use Wheel View:**
- Initial brainstorming
- Finding patterns quickly
- Client presentations (visual impact)
- Seeing the "big picture"

**When to use List View:**
- Copying specific questions
- Detailed analysis
- Exporting to spreadsheet
- Processing large result sets

## Interpreting the Data

### Understanding Search Intent from ATP Results

**Informational Intent (Questions):**
- "what is [keyword]"
- "how to [do something]"
- "why does [thing happen]"

**Content type:** Blog posts, guides, tutorials, FAQ sections

**Navigational Intent (Prepositions with brand names):**
- "[keyword] with [tool name]"
- "[keyword] for [specific use case]"

**Content type:** How-to guides, integration docs, use case pages

**Commercial Investigation (Comparisons):**
- "[keyword] vs [alternative]"
- "[keyword] or [option]"
- "best [keyword] for [use case]"

**Content type:** Comparison pages, buying guides, alternatives pages

**Transactional Intent (Product-focused):**
- "[keyword] price"
- "[keyword] discount"
- "buy [keyword]"

**Content type:** Product pages, pricing pages, landing pages

### Identifying Content Opportunities

**High-Opportunity Indicators:**

1. **Question clusters**: 5+ related questions on one subtopic
   - Indicates strong interest in that angle
   - Consider dedicated deep-dive content

2. **"How to" questions**: Practical, actionable queries
   - People want step-by-step guidance
   - Create tutorial content

3. **"Best/top" queries**: Evaluation-stage searches
   - Buyers comparing options
   - Create comparison or listicle content

4. **"vs" comparisons**: Direct competitive searches
   - High commercial intent
   - Create versus pages

**Low-Opportunity Indicators:**

1. **Overly specific edge cases**
   - "email marketing for underwater basket weavers"
   - Likely very low volume

2. **Nonsensical combinations**
   - ATP sometimes shows grammatically odd phrases
   - Not real search queries

3. **Single isolated questions**
   - No related questions
   - Might be outlier, validate volume first

## Exporting and Using Results

### Export Options

**CSV Export (All Plans):**
1. Click "Download CSV" button
2. Opens spreadsheet with all questions
3. Organized by category
4. Ready for manipulation in Excel/Google Sheets

**What you get:**
- All questions listed
- Category labels
- No search volumes or metrics
- Requires cleanup (duplicates, formatting)

**Typical CSV structure:**
```
Category, Question
Questions, what is email marketing
Questions, how to start email marketing
Prepositions, email marketing for beginners
```

### Processing Exported Data

**Step 1: Clean the Data**

Remove:
- Duplicate entries
- Nonsensical combinations
- Overly long-tail questions (15+ words)
- Questions with poor grammar

**Step 2: Categorize by Intent**

Group questions:
- Informational (what, how, why)
- Commercial (best, top, vs)
- Navigational (with brand names)
- Transactional (price, buy, cost)

**Step 3: Prioritize for Content**

Since ATP doesn't show volume, prioritize by:
1. **Business relevance**: Directly related to your offering
2. **Assumed popularity**: Common sense check
3. **Competitor gaps**: Questions competitors don't answer
4. **Content feasibility**: Can you create authoritative content?

**Step 4: Validate with Volume Data**

Use complementary tools:
- Google Keyword Planner (free, volume ranges)
- Ahrefs ($129/month, precise volumes)
- SEMrush ($139.95/month, precise volumes)

Copy ATP questions into these tools to get search volumes and difficulty scores.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Creating Content for Every ATP Question Without Volume Validation

**The problem:**
ATP shows autocomplete suggestions, not search volumes. Some questions appear but have &lt;10 monthly searches.

**The fix:**
- Always validate ATP questions with volume tools
- Set minimum volume threshold (e.g., 50 searches/month for blog posts)
- Use business relevance as secondary filter

### Mistake 2: Separate Pages for Nearly Identical Questions

**The problem:**
- "how to use email marketing"
- "how to do email marketing"
- "how to start email marketing"

Creating 3 separate pages causes keyword cannibalization.

**The fix:**
- Group similar questions into one comprehensive page
- Use question variations as H2/H3 headings
- Target all variations from single authoritative page

### Mistake 3: Ignoring Prepositions and Comparisons

**The problem:**
Focusing only on questions, missing commercial intent keywords in prepositions and comparisons.

**The fix:**
- Review all ATP sections (questions, prepositions, comparisons, alphabetical)
- Prepositions often reveal use case content opportunities
- Comparisons indicate buying-stage searches (high value)

### Mistake 4: One-Time Research Instead of Ongoing Monitoring

**The problem:**
Running ATP once, using data for months/years as search behavior changes.

**The fix:**
- Monthly ATP checks for trending topics
- Quarterly checks for evergreen topics
- Annual comprehensive research for core keywords
- Use automation (Apify actor) for regular monitoring

### Mistake 5: Using ATP Data Without SERP Analysis

**The problem:**
Creating content based on ATP questions without checking if you can actually rank.

**The fix:**
Before creating content:
1. Google the ATP question
2. Check domain authority of top 10 results
3. Assess content quality and depth
4. Determine if you can create superior content
5. Skip questions dominated by high-authority sites if you're new
