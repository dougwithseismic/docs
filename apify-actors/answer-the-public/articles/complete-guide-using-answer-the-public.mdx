# How I Actually Use Answer the Public (Without Paying $99/Month)

Answer the Public is everywhere in SEO Twitter. Those colorful wheel charts. The "revolutionary" keyword research. The "$99/month will change your content strategy" claims.

Here's what nobody tells you: The tool is fine, but you're probably using it wrong. And you definitely don't need to pay for it.

## What Answer the Public Actually Does

Let's be clear about what you're getting. Answer the Public scrapes autocomplete suggestions from search engines and shows them in a pretty visualization. That's it.

When you search "email marketing," it shows you:
- Questions: "how to start email marketing"
- Prepositions: "email marketing for beginners"
- Comparisons: "email marketing vs social media"

This data comes from Google's autocomplete API. The same API you can access yourself if you know what you're doing.

The value isn't the data - it's the presentation and organization.

## The Free Plan Reality Check

3 searches per day. That's the free limit.

For casual browsing, this works. For actual content planning, it's useless. To research 30 keywords for a content calendar would take 10 days. That's not a workflow - that's punishment.

## How I Actually Use It

Here's my real workflow after years of doing SEO and content strategy:

### 1. Start with Core Topics

I don't search random keywords. I start with 5-10 core topics that matter to the business.

For a SaaS product selling email marketing software:
- email marketing
- email automation
- email campaigns
- newsletter software
- email list building

That's it. Five searches that cover the entire product space.

### 2. Look for Question Clusters

I don't care about individual questions. I care about patterns.

If ATP shows 15 variations of "how to grow an email list," that's a signal. One comprehensive guide covering all those angles beats 15 mediocre blog posts.

Example from a real search:
- how to grow email list fast
- how to grow email list from scratch
- how to grow email list without ads
- how to grow email list for free

All of these get answered in ONE article: "How to Grow Your Email List: 12 Strategies That Actually Work"

### 3. Ignore the Volume Data (Because There Isn't Any)

ATP doesn't show search volumes. This is critical to understand.

A question appearing in ATP just means Google's autocomplete thinks it's relevant. Could be 10 searches per month. Could be 10,000.

You need a separate tool to validate volumes:
- Google Keyword Planner (free, imprecise)
- Ahrefs ($129/month, precise)
- SEMrush ($140/month, precise)

Anyone telling you to create content based purely on ATP data is either lying or hasn't actually done SEO.

### 4. Check Actual SERPs Before Creating Content

ATP shows what people search. It doesn't show if you can rank.

Before writing anything, I Google the actual question and check:
- Who's ranking? (If it's all DR90+ sites, forget it)
- What's the content quality? (Can I do better?)
- What's the search intent? (Does it match what I want to rank for?)

This 30-second check has saved me from writing hundreds of pointless articles.

## What Actually Works

After using ATP for years across dozens of clients, here's what delivers results:

### Use It for FAQ Sections, Not Full Articles

ATP questions make perfect FAQ content:
- Add to product pages
- Create FAQ schema markup
- Target featured snippets

One client added 20 ATP-sourced questions to their product pages with FAQ schema. Organic traffic increased 34% in 90 days just from featured snippet captures.

### Use Comparisons for Bottom-Funnel Content

The "vs" and "or" queries are buying-stage searches:
- "email marketing vs mailchimp"
- "drip or convertkit"

These aren't massive traffic drivers, but they convert at 5-8x the rate of informational queries.

### Use Prepositions for Use Case Pages

Searches like "email marketing for ecommerce" reveal specific use cases. These become:
- Landing pages for paid campaigns
- Industry-specific content
- Sales enablement material

## Common Mistakes I See

### Mistake 1: Creating Separate Posts for Nearly Identical Questions

I've seen this hundreds of times:
- "how to use email marketing" (one post)
- "how to do email marketing" (separate post)
- "how to start email marketing" (another post)

This is keyword cannibalization. You're competing with yourself.

Instead: One comprehensive guide answering all three angles.

### Mistake 2: Not Validating Search Volume

ATP shows "email marketing for underwater basket weavers" because it's in autocomplete somewhere. That doesn't mean anyone searches for it.

I once had a client create 40 articles from ATP suggestions without checking volumes. 38 of them got zero traffic. Ever.

Always validate with actual keyword tools.

### Mistake 3: Ignoring Search Intent

Just because a question exists doesn't mean you should answer it.

Example: "how much does email marketing cost"

If you sell email marketing software, this seems perfect. But Google shows calculator tools and comparison sites, not product pages. You'll never rank your product page for this query.

Check the SERP first.

### Monthly Research (30 minutes)

1. List 10-20 core keywords
2. Run Apify actor bulk search
3. Export results to Google Sheets
4. Filter for question clusters (5+ related questions)
5. Validate top 20 clusters with Ahrefs

### Content Creation (per article)

1. Pick a question cluster from research
2. Find 3-5 related ATP questions to answer
3. Check competitor content length and depth
4. Create comprehensive guide covering all angles
5. Add FAQ schema for related questions

### Optimization (quarterly)

1. Re-run ATP searches on core keywords
2. Identify new trending questions
3. Update existing content with new questions
4. Track which ATP-derived content drives traffic

<Accordion title="Can't I just use Google autocomplete directly?">
Yes, but it's tedious. ATP organizes hundreds of autocomplete suggestions in one place. The value is aggregation and organization, not proprietary data. The actor automates this even further with bulk processing.

<Accordion title="What about AlsoAsked or AnswerSocrates?">

<Accordion title="Should I create content for every ATP question?">
God, no. Validate volumes first. Check if you can actually rank. Make sure search intent matches your goals. Most ATP questions aren't worth targeting.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How often should I run ATP searches?">
Depends on your niche. Trending topics: monthly. Evergreen topics: quarterly. Most people over-research and under-execute. Research less, write more.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do I need Ahrefs if I have Answer the Public?">
ATP shows questions. Ahrefs shows if anyone actually searches for them, how hard they are to rank for, and who's currently ranking. They're complementary, not alternatives.
</Accordion>
